APPLIED MACHINE LEARNING

Can a model learn
the wind tunnel?

Train a radial-basis-function (RBF) regression model on simulated aerodynamic data, evaluate it on unseen angles, then compare its lift and drag predictions with the original physics approximation.

Scientific limitation: All data here are synthetic and generated by the original simplified equations. Low error shows how well the model reproduces those equations—not real-world aerodynamic accuracy, CFD, experimental verification, or novel physical discovery.

01 / Train and test

The model uses Gaussian radial basis features of angle of attack and ridge regression. Coefficients are learned from 84 training angles; 36 separate angles are held out for testing. A fixed seeded shuffle makes the comparison reproducible.

What was learned?

The model is trained on paired inputs (angle of attack) and targets (CL, CD). It learns a nonlinear mapping through Gaussian basis functions. We apply the known dynamic-pressure relationship to convert predicted coefficients into forces. Wind speed, density, and area therefore remain in the physics scaling rather than being learned from scratch.

Unlike a lookup table, the regression generalizes between training angles. Unlike a validated aerodynamic predictor, it does not have measured wind-tunnel data, airfoil-shape features, Reynolds-number corrections, or uncertainty from real experiments. For genuine independent validation, train against published airfoil polar data and test on a fully separate dataset.
